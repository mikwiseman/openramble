# OpenRamble 0.37.0

# Words that cannot be pasted wait where you work

- If a paste cannot land, your words now stay on the dictation panel, in full, with the reason and one action. Before, a notice said the text was saved in the menu and left after four seconds.
- Tap your dictation key to paste them once you are in a text field. While a password field or a terminal protects typing, the tap copies them instead, and your own ⌘V pastes.
- Later, or Escape, keeps the words in History. Holding the key starts a new dictation.
- While the panel shows your words, it is left out of screenshots and screen recordings.

# The last take

- ⌃⌘V pastes your newest dictation into the app in front. While typing is protected it copies instead. You can change or clear the shortcut in Settings → General.
- If another app of yours uses ⌃⌘V, such as Paste Special in Excel or Word, OpenRamble now takes that shortcut. Change or clear it in Settings → General to give it back.
- The menu now starts with the last take. It replaces Insert Last Dictation, Copy Last Dictation and Recent Dictations.

# A new design

- The dictation panel is one piece of Liquid Glass on macOS 26 and later, and the system material on macOS 14 and 15. It grows to fit what it shows.
- What you said is set in a serif wherever it appears: on the panel, in the Library and in the first note. The app's own text stays in the system font.
- Settings has one page per topic with a switcher at the top. The dictation key is drawn as a key, and it goes down when you hold yours.
- The Library replaces the Recordings window. Dictations and recordings share one list by day, with one search: a dictation is found by its words, a recording by its name. Dictation History has moved here from Settings, which keeps the number to keep and Delete All.
- Setup starts with your dictation key on screen, and it answers your key before you grant anything.
- In hands-free dictation the panel shows the key that ends it.
- When you let go of the key, the panel stays gray, and turns blue only if writing your words down takes a while.
- In the menu bar, something that waits for you is an orange bar instead of an orange dot, so it cannot be mistaken for the Mac's microphone indicator.

Your voice and text still never leave your Mac.
