# OpenRamble 0.3.4

## The model download survives a bad connection

A single dropped connection anywhere in the 483 MB download used to end the
whole installation, and the only thing you could do was start over. On a flaky
link — a VPN, a hotspot, a hotel — that could happen every time.

Each source now gets several attempts before the mirror is tried, and a transfer
that drops picks up where it stopped instead of restarting the 425 MB file. If a
source hands over a damaged file, the mirror is used; that is what the mirror was
always for, but until now it could not help with damaged content.

A full disk is no longer mistaken for a network problem. It used to be retried
six times — about 2.7 GB of downloads — and then reported as a connection
failure. It now says what is actually wrong, once.

## A long dictation no longer freezes the app

Restoring punctuation after the term booster ran on a full matrix, so an
hour-long note took about 17 seconds of frozen app and half a gigabyte of memory
after you released the key. It now takes a tenth of a second.

## The live preview shows what you are saying

It used to show the last line and a half — a fragment, with no way to tell
whether the rest had been heard. It now keeps several sentences on screen.

## Updates arrive on their own

OpenRamble checks for updates automatically now, so a fix reaches you without
you having to go looking for it. You can turn it off in Settings → General.

Two things stay off regardless: nothing about your Mac, its system version or
language is sent with the check, and nothing installs by itself — an update
downloads and installs only after you click.

## Smaller things you will notice

- The menu bar shows the WAI mark at rest, and switches to a microphone the
  moment it is listening or something needs your attention.
- Settings open in front of what you were working in, instead of behind it.
- Escape during insertion no longer claims the dictation was cancelled when the
  text had already landed.
- Two dictations in quick succession no longer overwrite what you had copied.
  Previously the second one could leave the first dictation on your clipboard in
  place of your own content.
- If your clipboard cannot be put back after a paste, OpenRamble now says so
  instead of failing silently.
- A microphone that records nothing — muted, or a device that went away — now
  tells you, instead of dropping the dictation without a word.
- Code you dictate inside brackets or Russian quotation marks is left alone.
  `«TextPipeline.Output»` used to come out as `«TextPipeline. Output»`.
- Learning from your edits reads the field at 8 and 25 seconds, which is what
  the settings said all along; it was reading at 8 and 33.
