# OpenRamble 0.26.0

OpenRamble now records meetings. A new Recordings window records your microphone and, when you allow it, whatever your Mac is playing — which is how the other people in a call end up in the transcript. The transcript builds while the meeting is still running, labelled You and Others, and you can save or share it afterwards. Everything stays on this Mac, as before.

# Before you install

Recording the other side needs a permission macOS calls System Audio Recording. It is asked the first time you record, never at launch, and dictation works exactly as before without it. On macOS 14.0 and 14.1 the permission does not exist, so recordings capture your microphone only and the app says so.

Recording a meeting captures the other participants as well as you, and OpenRamble does not tell them. In many places recording a conversation without everyone's consent is illegal. Asking is your responsibility; the app will not do it for you.

Recordings are kept until you delete them, at about 230 MB an hour. The Recordings window shows the total, and deleting one moves it to the Trash.

# What changed

- A Recordings window, Command-0, with the list on the left and the transcript on the right. One red button starts recording; the chevron beside it records your microphone only, for a private note while something is playing, and that choice is never remembered.
- Recordings have no length limit. A dictation is still capped at five minutes; a recording is written straight to disk and bounded only by the space you have.
- The transcript appears while you record, a paragraph at a time, by the same local model that handles dictation. A dictation you start mid-meeting always takes the engine first and the transcript catches up afterwards.
- Your microphone and the Mac's audio are kept apart as the left and right channels of one file, so who said what is a fact about the recording rather than a guess. The file plays in QuickTime with you in one ear and the other side in the other.
- Without headphones the speakers reach your microphone loudly enough to be transcribed twice. OpenRamble recognises the second copy by its shape rather than its loudness and keeps it out of your paragraphs. Headphones remove the problem entirely, and the app says so while you record.
- A tap on the Mac's audio can fail by returning silence rather than an error, which is what happens on AirPods, on AirPlay, and when the permission was refused. OpenRamble plays an inaudible tone when a recording starts, watches for sound, and tells you what it knows: that it is checking, that it is capturing, or that nothing has arrived. A recording whose other side was never heard is filed as such and stays labelled.
- Recording never waits for that permission and never stops because of it. Nor does it stop because transcription failed, the model is missing, or a device changed mid-call.
- Save writes the transcript as Markdown or the audio as a compact m4a. Share hands both to whichever app you pick. A recording that captured only your microphone says so inside the file, where it is still true after it has been forwarded.
- An interrupted recording is repaired and published at the next launch, with whatever had not been transcribed picked up from where it stopped.
- Quitting mid-recording asks first, then seals and saves what you had.

Recognition and transcription remain entirely local. Nothing is uploaded, and no other app is told you are recording.
