# OpenRamble 0.23.0

Dictation no longer waits in a queue when your Mac is busy.

## What was wrong

Recognition sometimes took seconds instead of a moment, at random. The speech
model was never the slow part — it finished in a fraction of a second every
time. The dictation was simply waiting for a turn.

Inference ran on the same small pool of threads that everything else in the app
shares, and that pool has one thread per processor core. When your Mac was busy,
a dictation could sit waiting for a free thread while the work itself was ready
to go. Measured on a loaded machine: thirty seconds of waiting for one and a
half seconds of recognition.

## What changed

Recognition now runs on a thread of its own, so a busy Mac no longer puts your
words in a queue behind everything else.

The level meter that animates while you speak also stopped competing with the
dictation it was drawn for. It updated on every fragment of audio — about twenty
times a second — and each update went through the same place the recognition
does. It now updates as fast as the screen can show it and no faster, which
looks identical and leaves the way clear.

## The trade, stated plainly

Recognition of very long recordings is roughly a tenth slower than before — a
three-minute recording now takes about four seconds rather than three and a
half. That is the cost of the separate thread, and it buys the removal of waits
measured in tens of seconds. Tenths of a second traded for tens of seconds.

## Also

The menu shows the shortcut for copying your last dictation, in its own entry
rather than hidden inside a submenu.
