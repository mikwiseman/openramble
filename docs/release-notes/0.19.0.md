# OpenRamble 0.19.0

Recognise a recording again, after you have taught the dictionary something.

## Run it again

Every dictation in History that still has its audio gains a button that
recognises it again. The words the model heard do not change — but what they
turn into does, once you have added a replacement or fixed a spelling. This is
how you see that, without saying the sentence a second time.

The new text replaces the old. The recording stays, so you can do it as often
as you like, and its star stays too — re-running a dictation you asked to keep
must not quietly stop keeping it. A run that fails changes nothing, rather than
blanking a record of something you actually said.

One at a time: the speech model takes one request, and a queue of them is a
queue nobody asked for.

## Reading the recording no longer holds up recognising it

A dictation that took eleven seconds spent a tenth of a second in the speech
model. The rest went into opening and decoding the recording — work that was
done on the same small pool of threads the dictation itself was waiting on, so
a slow moment on disk became a slow dictation.

The timer that should have caught this was measuring just after that step
instead of just before it, and faithfully reported a wait of zero. It measures
the whole wait now, so a number that says zero means zero.
