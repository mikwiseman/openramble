# OpenRamble 0.25.0

Long dictations now become text almost as soon as you let go of the key. The recording is recognised in pieces while you are still speaking, so at the end only the last few seconds are left to do.

# What changed

- A take is cut at your natural pauses and each piece is recognised during the recording. Measured on real dictations: a one minute take went from 1.71 s after key-release to 0.08 s, and a two minute take from 4.42 s to 0.16 s. The longer the dictation, the larger the difference — the old single pass got disproportionately slower as recordings grew, and that is exactly what this removes.
- Short dictations are unchanged. They produce a single piece and take the same path they always did, so there is nothing new to go wrong on the takes that were already fast.
- Cuts are only ever placed inside a pause, never through speech. If you talk without pausing, nothing is cut and the take behaves exactly as it did before. Latency is what degrades, never your words.
- The dictation log now reports how many pieces were recognised early, so a fast take can be explained rather than guessed at.

# On mixed-language dictation

0.24.4 described a limitation: the model picks one language for a whole recognition, so a take mixing two languages is written in whichever one dominates. Recognising the take in smaller pieces narrows that considerably, because each piece now gets its own decision. It narrows it rather than removing it — a switch between languages still has to fall near a pause to be isolated cleanly.

Recognition remains fully local and offline. No dictated text or audio is added to diagnostics.
