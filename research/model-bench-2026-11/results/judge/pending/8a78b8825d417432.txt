I fixed both recording bugs and committed the work as `09f283d` on the current branch. It is not pushed. The cause of both was in `Packages/DictationCore/Sources/DictationCore/MeetingSegmentPolicy.swift`.

**1. Last words before a pause were dropped.** When there is silence before anyone speaks, the policy moves the segment start forward and keeps one second of that silence. But it reset its inner pause detector to zero instead of telling it about the kept second. The pause detector measures its cut from the segment start, so every cut after a silent start or a long pause landed one second too early, inside the last words. The next segment then started after those words, so they ended up in no segment at all. The new test shows it: speech up to 15 s, but the segment ended at 14.35 s. The fix is one line: the pause detector is now told about the kept second of silence.

**2. The other person's quiet speech got no text.** Both channels used the microphone's speech threshold of 0.02, which exists to sit above room noise. The other side's audio is captured digitally from what the Mac plays, so it is exactly zero between words and has no room noise. Quiet remote speech under 0.02 was treated as silence and never transcribed. The other side now has its own threshold of 0.005, the quietest level the speech model was measured to transcribe (the measurement is noted in `SilentTakePolicy`). **Your microphone keeps 0.02**, so speaker sound leaking into it stays out of your paragraphs. A new test plays quiet remote speech that leaks into the microphone and gets one segment for the other side and none for you.

**One decision you should know about.** When a call recording starts, the app plays an inaudible 50 Hz tone to check that it can hear the Mac's audio. That tone was at 0.01, above the new threshold, and a test showed it would become a 5-second "Others" segment at 0:00 in every call. I lowered the tone to 0.0025. That is still five times the level the check needs, and half the new threshold. The one risk: the old level was measured on real hardware and the new one was not. If some Mac filtered the tone more than expected, the worst case is a false "other side isn't being recorded" warning that clears as soon as someone on the call makes a sound. A short check on a real Mac would settle it.
